How to Watermark Photos: Placement, Size and Opacity Guide

How to place, size and set opacity on a photo watermark: nine position

2026-09-17 · ~1100 words · 5 min read

Watermarking looks trivial — type some words, drop them on the image. But anyone who's done it knows the three real parameters (position, size, opacity) are easy to get wrong in both directions: a mark that's invisible does nothing, and one that's too loud ruins the photo. Here are settings you can copy directly.

1. Position: nine cells, each with a trade-off

PositionCrop-away costHides subjectVerdict
Bottom rightHighNo✅ Best default
Bottom leftHighNo✅ Good, suits left-weighted compositions
Top rightHighNoFine, but visually less settled than bottom right
Top leftHighNoFine for unusual compositions
Dead centreLowSevere⚠️ Only at very low opacity
Edge midpointsLowNoWeakest visually — avoid

Why bottom right wins

  1. It's where the eye lands. People scan an image and settle at the bottom right, so a mark there does its "notice me" job most reliably.
  2. It's usually low-value real estate — sky, ground, blurred background. A mark there doesn't cover the subject.
  3. Cropping it out costs composition. The bottom right is normally where the frame resolves, so removing it unbalances the shot.

Why centre is a trap

The centre is the visual focus — a watermark there is a stamp on the subject's face. If you must, drop opacity below 20%, but at that point it barely registers as a mark at all. Both directions lose.

2. Size: as a percentage of image width

Never use a fixed pixel size. A 48px mark is right on an 800px image and invisible on a 6000px one. Size it as a percentage of the image width.

% of widthEffectUse for
< 2%Unreadable❌ pointless
2–4%Restrained, subtlePortfolios, fine-art prints
4–8%Legible without shouting✅ recommended
8–15%AssertiveAnti-theft, strong attribution
> 15%Dominates❌ ruins the image

3. Opacity: 50–70% is the sweet spot

OpacityEffect
10–30%Barely visible — fails to mark anything
50–70%✅ Clearly readable, not distracting
80–100%Harsh, sticker-like, visibly damages the photo

On busy backgrounds (foliage, crowds) push toward 70–75% for legibility; on large flat areas, 50% is plenty.

4. The most overlooked setting: the outline

This is the detail that matters most in practice and gets skipped most often.

White watermark text on a light background simply disappears — white sky, white walls, snow, pale desktops are all worst cases. Black text on dark backgrounds fails the same way in reverse.

The fix is an outline: draw a semi-transparent dark (or light) edge around the glyphs so the mark is identifiable regardless of what's behind it.

In Canvas it's one extra step: strokeText (the outline) before fillText (the fill).

6. Three common mistakes

Mistake 1 — pure white at 100% opacity

The classic beginner watermark: fully opaque white, oversized. The mark ends up louder than the photo. Lowering opacity helps more than shrinking the font.

Mistake 2 — putting it on the subject

Faces, product subjects, the focal point — a mark there destroys the image's usability. The point of a watermark is to state provenance, not to wreck the work.

Mistake 3 — only fixing visibility after you notice it's missing

Turn the outline on first, then tune everything else. That way no image surprises you later.
